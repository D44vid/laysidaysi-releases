Why focus matters · Nov 21, 2026

You don't need more time. You need your time back.

Your attention is the most valuable thing you bring to your work. Here's why it's worth protecting, and how Laysidaysi helps you protect it.

(01)

Your attention is under pressure

You're not imagining it. In studies by Gloria Mark at UC Irvine, the average time people stayed on one screen at work fell from about 150 seconds in 2003 to about 47 seconds in more recent years. After an interruption, getting fully back into a task can take close to half an hour.

Here's the honest part: about half of those interruptions we start ourselves. Apps and feeds are built to pull us from one thing to the next, and our habits do the rest.

(02)

Every switch costs more than it seems

  • Part of your mind stays behind. When you jump to a new task, some of your attention stays stuck on the last one. Researchers call this attention residue, and it makes the next task harder.
  • Ignored notifications still cost you. In one study, a call or text people didn't even answer disrupted their focus about as much as using the phone.
  • Multitasking doesn't make you better at it. Stanford researchers found that heavy media multitaskers were more easily distracted, not less.

A day full of small switches leaves you tired, even if you felt busy the whole time.

(03)

A focused mind is a happier mind

Harvard researchers checked in with about 2,250 people at random moments of their day. Their minds wandered almost half the time, and they were less happy while they did. Training your focus is also training presence: being fully in your own day.

(04)

Deep work is where real things get made

Cal Newport calls it deep work: concentrating without distraction on something that pushes your thinking. Writing, coding, studying, solving hard problems. It builds your skills and creates work that's hard to copy.

Deep work usually needs unbroken blocks of one to four hours, not a few minutes between pings. And as AI takes over more shallow tasks, the ability to think hard and long is arguably worth even more.

(05)

The good news: you can train it

Attention works like a muscle. It weakens with constant switching and grows with practice.

  • Two weeks of mindfulness training improved students' working memory and reading test scores, and their minds wandered less.
  • Neuroscientist Amishi Jha suggests about 12 minutes of practice a day, five days a week.
  • Regular aerobic exercise gives your attention a modest boost.
  • Deep work itself is practice: the more you do it, the easier it gets.
(06)

How Laysidaysi helps

Willpower runs out. A good environment doesn't. That idea is behind every part of Laysidaysi.

  • Fewer triggers, less fighting. Each profile allows only the apps and websites a block really needs, and social media stays out of the way.
  • A pause instead of autopilot. When you reach for something else, Laysidaysi kindly asks: “Do you need this right now?”
  • Phone away before you start. The pre-session screen reminds you to put your phone on Do Not Disturb and out of sight.
  • Real blocks for deep work. Start with 60 to 90 minutes for your most important task, and grow toward two or three hours.

  • No shame, ever. Drifting off is human. Every “Back to focus” counts.
